The kit teaches the order

The kit's trays are packed in build order, so the parts a lesson needs are the parts on top. A team lays out only the current tray zone, builds, and checks off — the box itself enforces the sequence and keeps small parts from wandering.

MAKER classes print ahead

In the MAKER edition, each stage's printed parts are printed before the lesson that builds them — the print queue runs one stage ahead of the lessons, so printing never holds a class up. COMPLETE classes skip the queue and start at the tray.

One rule runs through all 14 lessons: a stage is finished only when its checkoff passes. A team that finishes early helps another team or practises in the simulator — nobody builds ahead on unverified work.

Program Requirement · Read before Lesson 1

Applies Every session, Lessons 1–14 | Posted in the room

Sourccey is a real machine — about a metre tall, with powered arms, a lift, and a lithium battery. These five rules are not suggestions. The teacher states them in Lesson 1, posts them in the room, and enforces them in every session that follows.

1 · Soldering is teacher-led

One step in the whole build uses a soldering iron: the potentiometer wires, in Lesson 7. It is done teacher-led, with gloves and ventilation, exactly as the Assembly Manual shows. Students prepare, hold, and inspect; the teacher runs the iron. No other lesson involves soldering, and the iron is never left unattended or passed hand to hand.

2 · Battery discipline

The robot runs on a LiFePO4 battery pack. Charge it only with the chargers included in the kit — never a substitute charger. At every power-up, check the battery gauge before doing anything else, and say the reading out loud so the whole team hears it. A pack that is swollen, punctured, hot, or reading abnormally comes out of service and goes to the teacher immediately.

3 · No adhesives on structural joints

If a joint does not fit, the fix is mechanical: unbolt it and refit it. Glue and tape on a structural joint hide a misalignment, make the next stage harder, and turn a five-minute refit into a broken part. Adhesives stay out of the build unless the manual itself calls for them.

4 · Two-person lift, power off

Built, Sourccey weighs 15.88 kg. Moving it is always a two-person lift, and the robot is powered off before it is moved every time, even across the room. Lift from the base, never by the arms, dome, or lift.

5 · Driving sessions are supervised

Driving Sourccey (Lesson 10) is always supervised: one driver at a time, a cleared drive area, and the robot's off switch within the teacher's reach.

CARE OF THE MACHINE

Power off before hands-on work

Any time hands go inside the body — wiring, refits, inspection — the robot is off first.

Know the off switch

From Lesson 9 onward, every student must be able to find and use the power-off switch without looking it up. The teacher checks this; it is part of the Lesson 9 checkoff.

Servos are not hand-cranks

Outside the hand tests the lessons specify, joints are moved by the controls, not forced by hand.

Epoxy work follows the manual's PPE

The wheel housings (Lesson 1, Manual Stage 0) are bonded with structural epoxy. While it is uncured it releases fumes: nitrile gloves, a face mask rated for organic fumes, and a well-ventilated area, exactly as the manual's safety note requires — and the bonded housings cure for at least an hour before they carry any load.

End every session tidy

Parts back in their tray zone, cables as the manual routes them, build log updated. The next session starts from a known state.

You start by touring the finished robot and the box, then assemble the base plates and mecanum wheels that everything else stands on.

STEP BY STEP — IN THE MANUAL'S ORDER

  • 1

Open — the destination moment (5 min)

Before anyone builds, the teacher runs the finished robot through its task while the class watches: this is where the course leads — the machine they will build and then teach. Then meet the robot: what it is, and what the class will build and teach it.

  • 2

Unbox and inventory · Stage 0 prep

Trays out in build order; every part verified against the packing map checklist before any tools come out. A part that is missing is found now, not in Lesson 7.

  • 3

Build the four wheel housings · Stage 0 — wheel housings

Fasten each Wheel Motor into its Wheel Holder — (6) M3x5mm per housing. The holders are handed: FL & RR pair, FR & RL pair. The mecanum wheels look identical at first glance but are not — match each wheel to its holder against Figure 0.8, with the rollers oriented as the figure shows.

  • 4

Bond and fit the wheels — teacher-supervised epoxy · Stage 0 — wheel housings

Apply epoxy to the wheel motor shaft, fasten the wheel coupler with the set screw (the shorter of the two screws in the wheel kit), apply epoxy to the coupler's outer surface, then fasten the mecanum wheel with the center screw (the longer screw). Repeat for all four housings, then leave the epoxy to cure at least 1 hour before the housings are loaded. Epoxy safety per the manual: nitrile gloves, a face mask rated for organic fumes, and a well-ventilated area while it is uncured.

  • 5

Assemble the base plates and rear wall · Stage 4 — base

Fasten the wheel housing assemblies to the Level 1 Base Plates — (44) M3x16mm — and fasten the Linear Actuator Base to the plates — (8) M3x8mm. Thread all wheel motor wires through as you go. Then fasten the Battery Holder to the rear base plate — (4) M3x12mm — and the Level 1 Rear Wall to it — (4) M3x16mm.

  • 6

Check

Inventory signed off against the packing map; roll test and parts-naming round against the checkoff.

KEY SKILLS

Parts identificationReading a photo guideFastener basics

TEACHER NOTES

Prep: finished robot ready to perform its task for the destination moment; a packing map checklist per team; base tray zone laid out per team. Watch for: mecanum wheels fitted in the wrong orientation — the roller pattern must match the manual's photo, or the base will not roll true in every direction.

FINAL STEP — DONE LOOKS LIKE · THE CHECKOFF

Every part verified against the packing map checklist; base rolls freely in every direction; every wheel spins true; team can name 10 parts.

GOALMotor supports, bicep, forearm, wrist, gripper for the first arm.

MU EXPLAINS IT

You build the first arm section by section — motor supports, bicep, forearm, wrist, and gripper — handling the servos carefully as each joint goes together.

STEP BY STEP — IN THE MANUAL'S ORDER

  • 1

Open (5 min)

Degrees of freedom — count the joints on a human arm, then find the robot's five. Before any motor is touched, the manual's horn rule: every servo carries two horns — the Driven Motor Horn has teeth on the inside, the Idle Motor Horn does not — and both go on before installation unless a step says otherwise. Two motors are the exceptions, and the steps below flag them.

  • 2

Shoulder and bicep · Stage 10

Insert Motor 1 into the Arm Base. Fasten the Planets to the Shoulder — (4) M3x20mm, (4) Planet Bearings — then the Shoulder to Motor 1 — (8) M3x8mm — and the Motor 1 Support to the Arm Base. Insert Motor 2 into the Shoulder — do NOT attach the Driven Motor Horn to Motor 2 — fit the Motor 2 Support, then the Sun Gear with its Driven Motor Horn to Motor 2. Fasten the Bicep to Motor 2 — (4) M3x14mm — and the Bicep Ring Gear across Bicep and Shoulder — (6) M3x25mm.

Page 22 — Clinic method — work the table in order, power off before any fix

Troubleshooting Clinic (curriculum — after Lesson 9)

First power-up is where the build tells the truth. Work this table in order — symptom, check, fix — before changing anything else. Power off before any hands-on fix, per Safety & Care. The clinic rule: Stop and diagnose. A fault found here is the lesson, not an interruption of it — and no fix moves the class past a checkoff that has not passed. Power off before any hands-on fix, and restate the gauge and off-switch checks before power goes back on.

Page 22 — Clinic — a device does not report in

Troubleshooting Clinic (curriculum — after Lesson 9)

Symptom: A device does not report in. Check: Re-run Lesson 9’s device verification, device by device, out loud, from the top. Fix: Stop and diagnose — do not carry a silent device into calibration. The answer is almost always a Lesson 7 cable or an unset servo ID. In the manual, servo search and ID setup are Stage 2 (page 22): plug the servo into a Motor Arm Cable, select Search in the FD software — if a motor does not appear, the selected COM port is incorrect.

Page 22 — Clinic — servo ID conflict: wrong joint answers, or joints answer together

Troubleshooting Clinic (curriculum — after Lesson 9)

Symptom: Servo ID conflict — the wrong joint answers, or joints answer together. Check: Check the servo IDs against the motor-ID programming from Lesson 7 (Stages 1–3): IDs must be set before the arms connect. Fix: Reset the conflicting ID, verify it, and only then reconnect. In the manual, IDs are set in Stage 2 (page 22): select the motor in the FD software’s Programming tab and change the ID at address 5 (Figure 2.3, page 25). Robot Motors: 1 STS3215; 2–3 STS3250; 4–6 STS3215; 7 STS3215; 8–9 STS3250; 10–12 STS3215. Teleop Motors 1–12 STS3215-TELEOP.
